How it works

A short, deliberate sequence. Each step has to hold up before the next one begins.

  1. 1

    Observe

    Review public information about the business — its own website, listings, and other openly available material.

  2. 2

    Understand

    Separate what is actually known from what is only a hypothesis, and mark clearly what remains unknown.

  3. 3

    Demonstrate

    Build a bounded, concrete simulation of what one specific improvement could look like in practice.

  4. 4

    Discuss

    Compare that demonstration with how the business actually operates today, and decide together whether it is worth pursuing.

What makes Proofline different

The discipline matters more than the output. These are the rules the work follows.

  • Evidence before assumptions

    A claim about a business starts from something observable, not from a guess about what is probably true.

  • Unknown stays unknown

    When something cannot be established from the evidence, it is labelled as unknown rather than filled in.

  • No invented numbers

    No projected returns, no made-up metrics, no performance figures that have not actually been measured.

  • No pretending to know the inside

    Proofline does not claim to know how a business runs internally unless the business has said so.

  • A demonstration is a simulation

    Unless it is genuinely connected to real systems, a demonstration is described plainly as a simulation.

  • People decide

    Humans stay responsible for decisions that carry real consequences. The work informs those decisions; it does not replace them.

What Proofline focuses on now

Right now, Proofline focuses on operational opportunities in service businesses — starting with how inbound requests are received, sorted, and qualified.

It is a deliberately narrow starting point. That kind of workflow is common, it can be compared against a business's own process, and it is straightforward to demonstrate concretely. The approach is meant to widen over time, one well-understood area at a time.
