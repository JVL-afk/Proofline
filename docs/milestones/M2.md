# Milestone M2 — Opportunity Engine

**Status:** Complete — bounded local M2 implementation validated
**Design updated:** 2026-08-16
**Completed:** 2026-08-16
**Closure reviewed:** 2026-08-16
**Boundary:** Opportunity hypotheses, deterministic economics, transparent factor snapshots, and
mandatory human review only. M3 artifacts and external actions are excluded.

## Purpose

Transform provenance-backed M1 research into conditional, reviewable opportunity hypotheses while
preserving this semantic chain:

`Evidence -> Observation -> Inference -> Opportunity Hypothesis -> Assumption -> Economic Run -> Score Snapshot -> Human Review`

These concepts are not interchangeable. Unknown internal facts remain unknown. No output may claim
actual response delay, lead volume, conversion, revenue, cost, lost revenue, tooling, staffing, or
financial impact without verified business-specific input.

## Bounded-context ownership

| Context | Owns | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Research and evidence | Canonical M1 evidence and provenance | Describes public surfaces; does not interpret business value |
| Opportunity intelligence | Evidence-linked M2 observations, versioned definitions, inferences, alternatives, gaps, hypothesis revisions | Does not fetch public pages or perform financial calculation |
| ROI and scoring | Typed assumptions, formula versions, economic runs, factor/ranking snapshots | Deterministic calculation only |
| Review and approval | Review tasks and immutable, version-bound hypothesis decisions | Only a human decision can accept a hypothesis |

M2 consumes M1 through typed evidence/observation catalog ports. It must not import M1 adapter or
persistence models. A shared local database does not become an integration API.

## Domain contracts

### Evidence

M1 remains the canonical owner. M2 uses an immutable reference containing evidence, workspace,
business, research run, snapshot/version, source URI, capture time, content hash, locator,
extractor/version, freshness/supersession state, and prompt-injection flag.

### Observation

A normalized statement of what was directly observable on a captured public surface. It contains a
controlled predicate, typed value, observation scope, normalizer/version, timestamps, verification
state, and `SUPPORTS`, `CONTRADICTS`, or `CONTEXT` evidence relations.

An observation needs at least one evidence item. Bounded absence may be recorded only as, for
example, “no matching mechanism was observed in the captured pages and crawl scope.” It is never
proof of an internal absence.

### Inference revision

A conditional interpretation derived from observations. It records its proposition, supporting and
contradictory bases, deterministic rule or mock-reasoner version, reasoning summary, alternatives,
important gaps, and a heuristic confidence band.

Inference revisions are inspectable, revisioned, and rejectable, but do not require a separate
mandatory approval. Rejection invalidates dependent hypothesis revisions. A hypothesis reviewer
reviews the inference lineage as part of the mandatory hypothesis review.

### Opportunity hypothesis revision

A conditional candidate containing the definition/version, permitted problem wording, proposed
workflow class, supporting and contradictory lineage, alternatives, important gaps, feasibility
dependencies, assumption set, economic run, factor snapshot, and generator/configuration versions.

It is not a confirmed business problem. Acceptance always requires a human decision bound to this
exact hypothesis revision and its manifests.

### Information gap

Each important gap records:

- stable gap type and description;
- importance/priority: `CRITICAL`, `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, or `LOW`;
- affected component: `PROBLEM_EXISTENCE`, `CURRENT_PROCESS`, `DEMAND_VOLUME`,
  `RESPONSE_PERFORMANCE`, `QUALIFICATION`, `CONVERSION`, `CUSTOMER_VALUE`, `COST`, `FEASIBILITY`,
  `INTEGRATION`, or `OTHER`;
- economic effect: `BLOCKS_MODEL`, `MATERIALLY_CHANGES_MODEL`, or `NO_DIRECT_EFFECT`;
- whether it blocks hypothesis review readiness;
- suggested validation question for a future human conversation;
- resolution state, source, actor, and revision history.

The suggested question is review information only. M2 does not contact a business or create
outreach.

### Assumption revision

An append-only typed input with value state `KNOWN`, `PROPOSED`, `UNKNOWN`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
It carries value/range, unit, currency, time basis, source kind, provenance, verification state,
actor, reason, and expiry/validity metadata.

The initial executable source kinds are deliberately narrower: `VERIFIED_BUSINESS_INPUT`,
`USER_PROPOSED`, and `UNKNOWN`. Inputs without verified business-specific data remain `UNKNOWN` or
visibly `PROPOSED`; M2 embeds no benchmark/default values.

### Economic run

An immutable execution snapshot of a vetted formula version and exact assumption revisions. Status
is `COMPLETE`, `HYPOTHETICAL`, `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, `INVALID_INPUT`, or `FAILED`.

### Score snapshot

An immutable factor/ranking explanation. Until A-14/A-19 calibration exists, it exposes factor
bands and missing-data behavior, not percentages or a definitive headline Opportunity Score.

## Persistence model

The bounded M2 slice uses append-only records and current-revision pointers. Its SQLite adapter owns
`opportunity_analysis_runs`, `opportunity_observations`, `opportunity_inference_revisions`,
`opportunity_hypothesis_revisions`, `opportunity_information_gaps`,
`opportunity_assumption_revisions`, `opportunity_economic_runs`,
`opportunity_score_snapshots`, `opportunity_review_decisions`, and
`opportunity_review_invalidations`. Stable definition, rule, formula, and factor-config versions
are stored on their resulting records and manifests rather than represented as editable database
configuration. JSON columns store ordered immutable ID lists and transparent factor/dependency
values; they do not store executable rules.

A decision manifest binds the exact hypothesis, inference, assumption, formula, economic-run,
factor-config, and score-snapshot revisions. Later material changes create a new hypothesis revision
and invalidate prior acceptance.

## State transitions

- Analysis run: `PENDING -> RUNNING -> SUCCEEDED | INSUFFICIENT_DATA | FAILED`.
- Inference revision: `ACTIVE -> REJECTED | SUPERSEDED`; observations are immutable.
- Hypothesis: `CANDIDATE -> NEEDS_INFORMATION | READY_FOR_REVIEW -> ACCEPTED | REJECTED -> SUPERSEDED`.
- Economic run: `COMPLETE | HYPOTHETICAL | INSUFFICIENT_DATA | INVALID_INPUT`.
- Score snapshot: `COMPLETE | INCOMPLETE | INELIGIBLE`.

Only a human review decision may enter `ACCEPTED`. An inference does not need separate approval, but
a rejected inference cannot remain an active basis for a hypothesis.

## Deterministic reasoning architecture

Live AI reasoning is disabled throughout M2. Candidate generation and verification use versioned
deterministic predicates, contradiction rules, wording policies, and missing-data gates.

A mock reasoning port may return schema-valid fixture results to prove contracts, retries, lineage,
and failure behavior. Mock output is untrusted, may reference only supplied IDs, and cannot create
evidence, formulas, rules, or accepted hypotheses.

Opportunity definitions are reviewed manifests plus vetted deployed strategies, never uploaded or
generated executable code.

## Initial Commercial HVAC lead-response definition

### Identity and scope

- Stable key: `commercial_hvac.inbound_lead_response_qualification`.
- Scope: inbound service, repair, maintenance, replacement, quote, or consultation requests exposed
  on public digital surfaces.
- Excluded: outbound sales, reactivation, dispatch optimization, CRM replacement, autonomous
  booking, form submission, authenticated surfaces, and claims about actual internal performance.

### Required predicates

All must be satisfied before a hypothesis may be `READY_FOR_REVIEW`:

1. `business.industry.commercial_hvac_supported`: attributable public evidence indicates commercial
   HVAC services, commercial customers/facilities, or a clearly commercial HVAC service line.
2. `business.identity.matched`: evidence belongs to the same business and permitted domain.
3. `inbound_path.observed`: at least one legitimate public inbound path is observed, such as a
   service/quote/contact form, published service email, published service phone number, or public
   scheduling/request interface.
4. `inbound_path.service_intent`: the path is for prospective service/quote requests rather than
   careers, vendors, billing-only, emergencies-only outside scope, or general corporate information.
5. `evidence.integrity_ready`: supporting evidence is current enough for configured policy, has
   exact snapshot provenance, and is not invalidated by entity mismatch or extraction failure.

These predicates establish only that an inbound path is publicly offered. They do not establish
lead volume, response time, qualification quality, staffing, tooling, or conversion.

### Supporting predicates

- A visible “request service,” “request a quote,” “schedule,” or “contact service” call to action.
- A public form requesting limited lead details such as company, service need, location, urgency,
  or contact preference.
- Multiple commercial services, facility types, service areas, or maintenance offerings that imply
  heterogeneous inquiry-routing needs.
- Public emergency, after-hours, rapid-response, or availability language that makes response
  handling operationally relevant.
- Multiple inbound channels that could benefit from consistent intake and routing.
- Within the bounded capture scope, no public explanation of immediate acknowledgement,
  qualification, routing, or scheduling behavior was observed.

The last predicate is scoped absence only and receives lower evidentiary weight.

### Contradictory predicates

- Public evidence explicitly describes 24/7 staffed intake or live dispatch for the applicable
  channel.
- A visible, functioning public workflow performs structured qualification and immediate routing or
  scheduling.
- The site explicitly documents prompt acknowledgement and human follow-up expectations for the
  applicable request type.
- Current public material states that the business is not accepting the applicable inquiries.
- Evidence shows the observed path belongs to another entity or an unrelated service line.

Contradictions are retained. They can reduce a candidate to weak, send it to `NEEDS_INFORMATION`, or
make it ineligible under the definition version; they are never silently discarded.

### Disqualifying conditions

- Commercial HVAC identity cannot be supported.
- No qualifying inbound path is observed in the captured scope.
- The only observed channels are careers, vendors, billing, investor relations, or unrelated work.
- Entity mismatch, provenance failure, or stale/superseded evidence violates readiness policy.
- The proposed workflow would require prohibited autonomous contact, form submission, authenticated
  scraping, or sensitive/discriminatory qualification.
- A hypothesis can be formed only by asserting unobserved internal behavior as fact.

“No qualifying path observed” means this plugin lacks sufficient public evidence. It does not mean
the business has no inbound process.

### Alternative explanations

Every generated hypothesis considers at least these alternatives:

- Existing staff may answer calls or messages promptly.
- Existing CRM, call-center, answering-service, email automation, or routing may not be visible.
- The public form may route to a well-staffed dispatcher.
- Customers may predominantly use phone, referrals, portals, or established-account channels.
- The captured website may be stale or incomplete relative to current operations.
- A third-party scheduling or qualification path may exist outside the permitted capture scope.
- Capacity constraints may make faster intake undesirable at present.

### Initial information gaps

| Gap | Priority | Component | Economic effect | Suggested future validation question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly applicable inbound inquiries | Critical | Demand volume | Blocks model | “Approximately how many new commercial service or quote inquiries arrive in a typical month, and through which channels?” |
| Current acknowledgement/response distribution | Critical | Response performance | Blocks model | “How are new inquiries acknowledged today, including after hours, and what response times are typical?” |
| Qualified-inquiry definition and rate | High | Qualification | Material | “What makes an inquiry qualified for your team, and roughly what share meet that definition?” |
| Baseline conversion to booked work | Critical | Conversion | Blocks model | “Of qualified inquiries, what share typically becomes booked work?” |
| Customer/job value and value semantics | Critical | Customer value | Blocks model | “What value measure should be used—revenue, gross profit, or contribution—and what range is typical for applicable work?” |
| Channel mix and seasonality | High | Demand volume | Material | “How does inquiry volume vary by phone, email, web form, service area, and season?” |
| Current tools and routing | High | Current process | Material | “Which systems or services currently receive, route, and track new inquiries?” |
| Staffing, ownership, and handoff | High | Feasibility | Material | “Who owns first response and qualification, and when must a human take over?” |
| Integration/API availability | High | Integration | Material | “Which form, phone, email, CRM, scheduling, and dispatch systems would an approved workflow need to integrate with?” |
| Implementation and operating cost | Critical | Cost | Blocks model | “What implementation and ongoing cost constraints should the economic model use?” |

### Feasibility dependencies

- Authorized access to the applicable inbound channels and routing configuration.
- A defined qualification rubric, service area, capacity constraints, and human-handoff policy.
- Named operational ownership and after-hours escalation behavior.
- Compatible, approved form/email/phone/CRM/scheduling/dispatch interfaces.
- Data minimization, access control, retention, monitoring, and fallback procedures.
- Ability to avoid autonomous commitments, quotes, bookings, or unsafe emergency guidance.

Unless verified, each dependency is `UNKNOWN`; public website observations do not prove it.

### Permitted hypothesis wording

> Public pages show that the business invites commercial HVAC inquiries through [observed channels].
> A structured acknowledgement and qualification workflow could be evaluated for those channels.
> Current response performance, internal routing, lead volume, conversion, feasibility, and economic
> impact remain unknown pending business verification.

Conditional variants may name observed form fields, service types, service areas, or public
availability statements with citations. They may describe a risk or evaluation opportunity, not an
existing failure.

### Prohibited claims

- “The company responds slowly,” “waits until Monday,” or “misses leads.”
- “The process is manual,” “there is no CRM,” or “staff fail to follow up.”
- Any invented lead count, response time, conversion rate, close rate, customer value, margin, cost,
  savings, revenue, or lost-revenue figure.
- “AI will increase revenue by X,” “recover X leads,” or any unverified causal outcome.
- Absence of a visible chatbot/widget proves absence of automation or internal capability.
- A hypothetical economic output is actual loss, impact, ROI, or forecast.
- Recommendations to submit forms, contact the business, bypass login/CAPTCHA, or make autonomous
  service, pricing, safety, or scheduling commitments.

## Economic-model infrastructure

Formula versions define typed inputs, unit/currency/time dimensions, requiredness, valid ranges,
output semantics, allowlisted arithmetic graph, scenario rules, rounding, checksum, and publication
state. Monetary calculations use decimal arithmetic. Arbitrary code or `eval` is prohibited.

Input precedence is verified user/business input, direct observation where semantically valid,
approved benchmark, approved industry default, otherwise unknown. Benchmarks/defaults are always
`PROPOSED` until reviewed for the specific run.

Required unknowns yield `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`. A `HYPOTHETICAL` run is allowed only when every proposed
input is visibly labelled in the API/UI and result manifest. Its outputs must be labelled
“hypothetical scenario,” never “lost revenue,” “actual impact,” or a business forecast.

A-13 is accepted for this formula only by ADR-0008. Other formulas, currencies, value meanings,
benchmarks, and scenario families remain unapproved.

## Factor and ranking design before calibration

Each factor result exposes `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `UNKNOWN`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, its raw inputs,
rubric version, lineage, contradictions, rationale, and missing-data behavior.

Initial components are:

- evidence strength;
- hypothesis confidence;
- potential value;
- implementation feasibility;
- business/definition fit;
- important-unknown burden.

Missing-data behavior is explicit: `BLOCK`, `CAP_BAND`, `LOWER_BAND`, or `INCOMPLETE`. Evidence
strength and hypothesis confidence remain distinct. Potential value is `UNKNOWN` when no valid
economic run exists and is explicitly `HYPOTHETICAL` when based on proposed inputs.

Before A-14/A-19 calibration, the UI exposes no confidence percentage, weighted total, definitive
headline Opportunity Score, or statistically suggestive precision. A versioned deterministic rule
table may assign review priority `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `NOT_RANKABLE`, or `INCOMPLETE`; it affects
queue order only and cannot accept a hypothesis.

## API/UI surface

The `/api/v1` surface starts and retrieves analysis runs, returns the complete semantic bundle by
run or hypothesis, recalculates exact assumption revisions, records version-bound review decisions,
and rejects inference revisions. Analysis creation is idempotent. Recalculation, inference rejection,
and review require exact hypothesis-revision preconditions.

The local diagnostic UI displays the complete typed bundle, including lineage, contradictions,
alternatives, information gaps and validation questions, sourced assumptions, formula/config
versions, hypothetical labels, factor bands, and review state. It provides controls for explicitly
proposed scenarios and human accept/reject decisions; it is not a production experience.

## Initial deterministic fixtures

### Strong candidate

A correctly identified commercial HVAC business exposes commercial service pages, service areas,
phone/email, and a request-service form with company, location, service need, and urgency fields.
No visible public description of acknowledgement/routing is captured. Expected result: required
predicates pass; several supporting predicates pass; alternatives and critical gaps remain; a
carefully worded hypothesis reaches human review. Economics remains insufficient or visibly
hypothetical unless proposed inputs are explicitly supplied.

### Weak candidate

A commercial HVAC page exposes only a general phone number and email with limited service-intent
context. Expected result: evidence and observation lineage exists, but support is weak, unknown
burden is high, and review priority is `LOW`; no internal-process claim.

### Contradicted candidate

A commercial HVAC site exposes a request path and current public statements describing 24/7 staffed
dispatch plus a visible structured qualification/scheduling workflow. Expected result:
contradictions remain attached, the hypothesis is weakened or ineligible under the rule version,
and no claim that response automation is absent appears.

### Insufficient-data candidate

A captured site has ambiguous industry identity or no service-intent inbound path within the bounded
scope. Expected result: readiness fails or the plugin is ineligible; information gaps explain what
is missing; no hypothesis is accepted and no favorable missing-data defaults are used.

### Scoped-absence case

A commercial HVAC form is observed, but no public response-time statement, chatbot, scheduling
widget, or routing explanation is present. Expected result: the system may state only that those
features/statements were not observed in the captured scope. It must preserve “current response and
internal process unknown” and must not infer manual handling, slow response, missed leads, absent
CRM, or lost revenue.

Additional hostile variants place prompt-injection text inside otherwise valid pages. The text must
remain inert evidence and cannot change predicates, formula inputs, ranking, or workflow behavior.

## Test strategy

Tests cover semantic type separation; cross-workspace/entity rejection; exact lineage;
contradictions; inference rejection invalidation; scoped absence; immutable revisions; information
gap metadata; formula type/unit/currency validation; unknown/proposed input behavior; hypothetical
labelling; decimal/range reproducibility; factor bands and all missing-data policies; mandatory,
version-bound hypothesis review; idempotency/retry; authorization; prompt injection; and deterministic
fixtures with no uncontrolled websites or live AI.

## M2 exit criteria

- [x] The five initial fixtures deterministically produce their specified distinct outcomes.
- [x] The complete semantic chain is durable and retrievable through API/UI.
- [x] Every observation cites M1 evidence; every inference and hypothesis exposes its full basis.
- [x] Contradictions, alternatives, rejected inferences, and important gaps remain inspectable.
- [x] Every important gap records priority, affected component, economic effect, and a future
  validation question.
- [x] No fabricated internal metric, process claim, financial input, or business impact appears.
- [x] Unknown business-specific inputs remain `UNKNOWN`; proposed inputs remain visibly `PROPOSED`.
- [x] Hypothetical calculations are reproducible, completely labelled, and never presented as
  actual loss or impact.
- [x] Factor components, bands, rationale, and missing-data behavior are exposed without percentages
  or a definitive headline score.
- [x] Live AI is disabled; deterministic rules and mock adapters prove contracts and workflow only.
- [x] A human, version-bound review decision is the only path to hypothesis acceptance.
- [x] Revision, rejection, recomputation, idempotency, authorization, security, and failure tests pass.
- [x] No M3 behavior is present.

## Implemented local slice

The implementation uses typed core/application ports, append-only SQLite revisions, a leased local
analysis queue with expired-lease retry, deterministic Commercial HVAC rules, immutable economics
and factor snapshots, revision preconditions, and exact-manifest review decisions. New research,
assumption recomputation, or inference rejection invalidates prior review. These adapters are local
M2 mechanisms only and do not decide any production provider or deployment choice.

## Closure verification

The final closure review used Node.js 24, npm 11, Python 3.13, and uv 0.11.32 with the committed
lockfiles. Repository validation, Ruff lint/format, strict mypy, the complete M0–M2 test suite,
Python byte-compilation, diagnostic-UI JavaScript syntax validation, and `git diff --check` all
passed. The reviewed change set contains only intentional M2 documentation, contracts, local
adapters, API/UI integration, and deterministic tests. Live AI and all M3 capabilities remain
absent.

## Deferred to M3

Audits, demos, proposals, outreach or sending, CRM/calendar/email/SMS/phone integrations,
interaction/outcome tracking, mass discovery, autonomous agents, generated code, external artifact
publication, and review bypass are excluded.
