## Why N=1 still does not fit

From Attempt 2's raw output: one full first-contact email body for these
envelopes runs ~1,400–1,700 output tokens. The **claim manifest** — mapping every
substantive clause of subject and body to envelope `ref` ids, as JSON — adds
several hundred more. Body **+** full manifest in one response exceeds 2,000
tokens for `claude-sonnet-5` even at N=1. The body alone would fit; the body
plus the complete manifest does not.

This is not a thinking artifact (thinking is disabled and confirmed working) and
not model misbehaviour — it is the **"full first-contact email + complete claim
manifest in one ≤2000-token response"** contract meeting the 2,000-token hard
ceiling.

## Options (owner decision — each touches something the authorization fenced off)

1. **Two-call structure per candidate.** Call A returns the candidate
   (`candidate_id`, subject, body). Call B is given that exact candidate text
   back as inert data and returns **only** its claim manifest. Both responses fit
   well under 2,000 tokens; the full manifest contract is preserved (nothing
   slimmed); the validator still independently re-verifies. Cost: 81 candidates ×
   2 = **162 calls**, ≈ USD 3.5–4.5 (still under the USD 10 ceiling). Needs a new
   prompt template pair (`@4a` / `@4b`) and a new certification key — a call-
   structure change, so owner authorization required.
2. **Raise `max_output_tokens`** to ~6,000–8,000 so one candidate + full manifest
   completes in a single response (back to N=1, 81 calls). This is the smallest
   structural change and the only one that keeps a single-response candidate, but
   it lifts the §7 hard ceiling of 2,000 that has been held firm across all three
   attempts. Owner must lift it explicitly.
3. **Deterministic manifest reconstruction.** The provider returns only the
   candidate; the manifest is built deterministically from the body afterwards and
   the validator cross-checks as today. Keeps N=1 / 81 calls / 2,000 tokens. But
   it removes the *provider-declared* manifest, which is part of what the
   certification is meant to observe — arguably an alteration of certification
   semantics.
4. **Abandon the in-response-manifest design for M6.8-3** and re-scope.

Recommendation: **option 1 (two-call structure)**. It is the only option that
keeps the 2,000-token ceiling, the full manifest, the validator, the corpus, and
the safety thresholds all unchanged — it just splits generation and manifest
declaration into two bounded responses. 162 calls stays well inside USD 10.
