PROJECT_OWNER proposes Andrew as the human reviewer for this pilot. Andrew
is also the pilot's sender identity (`Andrew <andrew@proofline.business>`)

**Controlling rule: `docs/adr/0049-production-separation-of-duties.md`**
(Status: Accepted, 2026-08-18):

> "The initial production policy requires separate content review, contact
> stewardship, and one-send authorization; **prohibits self-authorization**;
> requires step-up, reason, expiry, exact preview, and one-message
> acknowledgment; and permits no hard-gate override."

This is implemented in code at
`packages/activation-core/src/opintel_activation/domain.py:247-249`
(`AuthorizationPolicyRelease.content_reviewer_separate` /
`.contact_steward_separate` / `.self_authorization_allowed`) and enforced at
`packages/activation-core/src/opintel_activation/application.py:917-919`.
The only concrete instantiation of this field anywhere in the project
(`tests/test_m65_activation_readiness.py:260`) sets
`self_authorization_allowed=False` — no exception, waiver, or narrower
scope for a "bounded pilot" exists anywhere in this codebase or its
governance docs.

Andrew serving as both sender/composer of the outbound message and its sole
human reviewer is exactly the self-authorization ADR-0049 prohibits, and the
ADR requires the reviewer role to be *separate* from the sender/composer,
not merely willing to accept responsibility. "M6.5 represents the policy but
provisions no identity system" (ADR-0049 Consequences) means there is no
live IdP enforcing this today — but the absence of technical enforcement is
not authorization to waive an Accepted architecture decision; this task
does not invent a new rule, it applies the one that already exists.
