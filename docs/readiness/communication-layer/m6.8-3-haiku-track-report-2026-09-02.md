## C. Attempt-7 HTTP-400 attribution

The 52/81 `anthropic HTTP 400` (`GENERATION_REFUSED`) family on M6.8-3 Attempt 7
is **consistent with account/billing exhaustion**, matching the owner's report
that the account ran out of credit mid-run and has since been recharged:

* Anthropic returns **HTTP 400 `invalid_request_error`** for a depleted credit
  balance (not 401/403/429/5xx). Attempt 7 saw exactly HTTP 400.
* The failures were **not scenario-specific** — 5–6 of 9 per scenario, evenly
  distributed; billing state is global.
* The onset was a **hard switch**: the probe + ~29 early calls (repeats r0–r3)
  succeeded, then a consistent 400 wall for the rest. A transient API
  degradation would be randomly interspersed, not a clean cutover.
* The first ~29 calls returning well-formed candidates rules out a request-shape
  defect.

The adapter now retains the non-secret provider error `type` and a truncated
`message` (`provider_error_type` / `provider_error_message`), and the HTTP-4xx
reason string carries the error type, so any future occurrence is
self-documenting. No credential is ever exposed or persisted. No further effort
was spent treating this as a communication-system defect — the evidence confirms
the credit explanation.

## M. Model comparison and the plain answer

**Cost.** Haiku 4.5 at the published USD 1.00 / 5.00 per-Mtok rate is ~2.6× cheaper
per call than the owner-confirmed Sonnet 5 rate (USD 2.00 / 10.00). A completed
81-call Haiku certification would have cost ≈ **USD 1.0–1.3** vs Sonnet Attempt 6's
USD 2.68 — roughly 55–60 % cheaper. Total Haiku spend this task: **USD ~0.237**
(18 calls + 2 probes).

**What Haiku does well.** The prose is genuinely good: concise (81–104 words —
the ≤130 body cap that defeated Sonnet on every Attempt-6/7 candidate is a
non-issue for Haiku, and the compactor never had to fire), specific,
evidence-grounded, no fabrication, no invented economics or response behaviour,
prompt-injection resisted both times, manifest-mismatch rate **0.0000**. Read as
a human, several of the nine would be plausible to put in front of an HVAC
decision-maker after review.

**Where Haiku falls short — reliability, not capability.** Across 18 real
generations Haiku was *inconsistent on the mechanical safety/contract
requirements*: it dropped a required disclosure clause on 1 scenario, omitted
required placeholder lines on ~3–7 of 9 depending on prompt version, produced 1
unparseable response, and did not reliably lead with the strongest evidence hook.
An evidence-bound transformer for a safety-critical outreach path needs the
disclosure and structural contract satisfied *every time*, not most of the time.
Tightening `@8` further to compensate would be optimising for the gate rather
than for genuine compliance (section L).

### Is Haiku 4.5 good enough to use instead of Sonnet 5 for this role?

**Not on this evidence — no.** The writing quality is close enough and the cost
is materially lower, but Haiku's *mechanical compliance is not consistent enough*:
it dropped a required disclosure on 1 of 9, missed required placeholders on
several, and failed to parse 1. For M6.8-3's evidence-bound, human-review-gated
outreach, the correct call is:

* **Sonnet 5 remains the target model.** Sonnet's own blocker (the 130-word
  overshoot) is now solved deterministically by `comm.candidate_compactor@1` +
  the `@7`/`@8` conservative targets; a Sonnet re-run on the remediated `@4`
  pipeline in a funded window is the shortest path to a real M6.8-3 result.
* A **third Haiku qualification is possible** but only warranted if the owner
  wants a Haiku track badly enough to accept a more prescriptive disclosure /
  placeholder template — and it should be judged by the same J bar, with no
  further gate-fitting.
