> 1. TDLR dataset 7358-krk7: business-only fields
>    Conclusion: APPROVED_WITH_CONTROLS.
>    The current TDLR dataset is publicly published through the Texas Open Data Portal, and the portal describes open data generally as information that can be freely used, reused, and redistributed.
>    The dataset page currently shows 18 columns, including the proposed business/license fields and, separately, owner names, telephone numbers, mailing addresses, and other fields.  The Texas Data Portals Resource Guide expressly says that the portal's Public License Type may be left as “-- No license --,” and explains that a license reduces legal uncertainty; in other words, absence of a dataset-specific license is not itself a statement that reuse is prohibited, but it does leave some uncertainty.
>    So, for the narrowly defined Phase 1 process, I would approve storing/reusing only:
>    license_type, license_number, business_county, business_name, business_city_state_zip, license_expiration_date_mmddccyy, and license_subtype
>    subject to the controls already specified: reject natural-person/sole-proprietor/ambiguous-name records before durable projection, require a high-confidence legal-entity suffix, and do not query or retain the owner, telephone, street/mailing, or geospatial/contact fields.
>    I would not characterize this as an unconditional license grant. The defensible basis is the state's open-data publication policy plus the dataset's public availability, with the absence of a dataset-specific license treated as a residual uncertainty rather than as a prohibition.
>    Recommended controls are to retain the dataset URL/identifier and access date as provenance, preserve only the approved business fields, avoid onward publication of excluded personal fields, and periodically re-check the dataset's metadata/license status. This is a source-governance determination, not a legal opinion.
> 2. Brave Search API: storing the derived first-party hostname Conclusion: REJECTED under the ordinary current Search API terms/plan. This one is much clearer. Brave's current Search API Terms of Use, last updated February 11, 2026, define the API output as “Search Results.” The terms expressly prohibit customers from storing, caching, or creating a database of Search Results, in whole or in part, except for transient storage required to operate the application. The current Brave documentation is even more direct: it says that retaining data received through the Search API is prohibited and that users who need to store results should contact Brave.  The standard pricing page currently identifies an Enterprise offering with custom terms, including bespoke agreements. The proposed “exact host only” does not clearly escape that restriction merely because it is derived from a result rather than the full result being stored. The hostname is itself information contained in the Search Result / URL, and the contract prohibits storing Search Results “in whole or in part.” Therefore: Under the ordinary plan/terms: do not persist the hostname in the roster. A separately approved later exact-host review could become permissible only after Brave expressly authorizes the needed storage right, for example through an applicable order form/custom agreement or other written authorization that clearly permits retention of the derived locator. The terms specifically contemplate Order Forms, and Brave advertises custom contractual arrangements for Enterprise. There is also an important related point: Brave's terms say it grants no rights to third-party content included in Search Results, so even authorized storage of a locator would not itself authorize copying the destination webpage's content.
